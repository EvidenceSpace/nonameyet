# EvidenceSpace vision and product principles

## Product promise

EvidenceSpace turns facts, files, conversations, and research into an organized, source-linked case workspace. A truthful AI copilot helps the user understand what is known, what is disputed, what may matter, what is missing, and what to do next—without silently taking control.

## North-star experience

A user should be able to enter an unfamiliar, emotionally difficult, or professionally complex situation and leave each session with:

- a clearer understanding of the current case state;
- original material preserved and easy to reopen;
- visible relationships between evidence, people, events, issues, and tasks;
- uncertainty and contradictory information shown rather than hidden;
- a short, justified next step;
- an auditable history of human and AI actions; and
- a controlled path to collaborate or seek licensed help.

The differentiator is not the largest feature list. It is a coherent loop from **capture → organize → understand → decide → act → review**, with provenance and user control at every stage.

## Audience adaptation

EvidenceSpace welcomes broad audiences without creating separate products:

- **Individual:** managing a personal matter or preparing for professional review.
- **Legal professional:** organizing a client matter and collaborating with a team.
- **Investigator or reviewer:** collecting, connecting, and evaluating information.
- **Student or educator:** learning through clearly labeled simulations.
- **Organization:** handling an internal matter with permissioned collaboration.

Onboarding asks how the user is working and adapts vocabulary, templates, guidance depth, default board frames, privacy explanations, and recommended actions. The underlying evidence and provenance model remains the same.

## Product principles

### 1. User-led, AI-assisted

The user owns facts, decisions, sharing, and irreversible actions. AI may suggest, explain, research, draft, and propose changes. It does not silently promote a suggestion, delete evidence, share a case, book a lawyer, or submit a filing.

### 2. Sources before confidence theater

Every material AI claim points to the case evidence or external legal/research source that supports it. Confidence is never used as a substitute for evidence. Unsupported output is rejected or labeled as an unverified hypothesis.

### 3. Contrary information is first-class

The product actively shows information that weakens the user’s preferred interpretation. AI separates supporting, contrary, missing, and disputed material and explains why each matters.

### 4. Progress measures organization, not victory

Case Home may show evidence processed, tasks completed, unresolved questions, pending reviews, deadlines, and research freshness. It must not manufacture a probability of winning, guilt, liability, authenticity, or legal strength.

### 5. Calm, professional interaction

The interface can feel polished, responsive, and motivating without turning a serious case into a game. Motion explains causality and orientation. Rewards, streaks, artificial urgency, celebratory language about allegations, and manipulative paywalls are prohibited.

### 6. Adaptive language without hidden simplification

Professional users can receive concise terminology; less experienced users receive definitions and examples. Both see the same sources, uncertainty, and limitations.

### 7. Privacy is visible control

Users can see who can access a case, what AI context is being used, what leaves the device, what will be shared with a lawyer, how long data is kept, and how to delete it. Important privacy behavior is not buried in terms.

### 8. Accessibility is core functionality

The board, chat, reports, dialogs, and AI actions must be usable by keyboard and assistive technology. Visual relationships on the canvas need an equivalent structured list or outline; color and position alone cannot carry meaning.

### 9. Honest jurisdiction handling

Every legal analysis displays governing jurisdiction, source date, source type, and coverage limitations. When jurisdiction or case classification is unclear, AI asks rather than guessing.

### 10. Human help remains human

The AI is a case copilot, not a licensed lawyer. Lawyer profiles represent real professionals only after verification. Consultation, engagement, and representation are distinct states.

## Required AI answer shape

For serious case analysis, the default response structure is:

1. **Assessment** — concise direct answer.
2. **Why** — reasoning tied to facts and law/research.
3. **Case evidence used** — exact source links or locators.
4. **Contrary material and weaknesses** — what cuts against the assessment.
5. **Legal or research sources** — jurisdiction, publisher/court, date, and link.
6. **Unknowns** — facts or authority still missing.
7. **Next recommended step** — one justified action.
8. **Action proposals** — optional, previewable workspace changes.

## Product success signals

Numbers and targets require a separately approved measurement plan. The product should measure, without capturing private case content:

- completion of first case setup and first evidence addition;
- time to first useful, source-backed AI answer;
- percentage of AI claims with valid reachable sources;
- proposal approval, correction, rejection, and undo rates;
- recovery from interrupted uploads and failed actions;
- successful collaboration invitations and resolved tasks;
- report review and export completion;
- lawyer search-to-booking funnel where legally permitted;
- accessibility, performance, crash, and sync reliability; and
- trust comprehension through research rather than dark-pattern engagement metrics.

## Glossary

- **Evidence:** an original or captured item with provenance; the label does not establish admissibility or authenticity.
- **Research source:** external material used for understanding or legal research, stored with publisher, URL, dates, and excerpt.
- **Case fact:** a user-entered or user-accepted proposition with source status.
- **AI finding:** a provisional analysis or hypothesis; not a confirmed fact.
- **Issue track:** a possible charge, claim, defense, remedy, complaint, or educational theory appropriate to the case.
- **Action proposal:** an AI-requested change awaiting preview and approval.
- **Case readiness:** transparent organization progress, never outcome prediction.
