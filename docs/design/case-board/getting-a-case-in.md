# Getting a case in, and the first impression

The prototype case was typed in by hand. A real case has to reach that level through normal use, without feeling like homework.

## Principle

**Nothing lands on the board without a person saying yes.** AI reads uploads and suggests; people confirm, fix or dismiss. Every suggestion links to the exact place it came from.

## The flow

1. **Drop things in.** Photos, PDFs, emails (forwarding address), screenshots, videos, voice notes. No forms first.
2. **We read them.** Extraction finds people, dates, amounts, places, and sentences that are claims. Each item shows its source line.
3. **Quick confirm.** One card at a time, or a short list. For example: "Joan Pike says their light was red. Add to *What colour was the van's light?* as supporting your version?" Choose **Add**, **Edit** or **Not now**.
4. **Immediate payoff.** Each confirmation visibly changes something: a point moves, a hole fills, a total updates. If confirming changes nothing, it isn't worth asking.
5. **Loose papers.** Anything not placed yet waits in Loose papers with what was found in it, so nothing is lost.

## First impression (first 5 minutes)

The first screen after sign-up must be **helpful before it's impressive**.

- Ask one plain question: "What happened?" Offer quick starts: car accident, unpaid money, landlord or deposit, work problem, something else.
- For urgent situations ("It just happened"), show a short, phone-friendly checklist first: stay safe, photos, the other driver's details, witness names and numbers, police reference, tell your insurer. One tap uploads photos.
- Then show the case **half-empty on purpose**: the legal elements for that case type, each with "what usually proves this". The gaps are the guidance.
- Show one next step, never a wall of features.
- No tour carousel. Explain things in place, the first time they appear, in one line.

## What the board looks like on day one

A few cards, the proof panel with mostly empty items, two or three deadlines, and a clear next step. It must look calm and intentional, not broken. Empty states say what goes there and how to add it.

## Light mode for small cases

Many people (small claims, a deposit) want "tell me what to do next" and a letter, not a board. Offer a simple view: the proof checklist, next step, papers and the letter. The board is one click away and holds the same data.

## Trust

- Suggestions are labelled "Suggested · not added" until confirmed.
- Every claim keeps its source and location.
- Wrong suggestions are easy to correct and never silently applied.
- Nothing is sent to anyone without the person pressing send.
